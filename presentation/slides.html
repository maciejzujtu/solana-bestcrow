Bestcrow

Crowdfunding for startups where money releases by rule, not by a Kickstarter-style operator.

Anchor program·Backer-weighted votes·Pro-rata refunds
Track: Finance Without Intermediariessolana-bestcrow
One sentence: we remove the crowdfunding operator's custody and its power to decide payouts. Everything that replaces it is in the on-chain program, not our backend.
Problem · who it hurts

Two people who don't know each other, and a platform both must trust

The creator

Needs capital before the product exists, but must use a platform that controls the campaign and payout flow.

The backer

Funds a stranger and risks losing control after the goal is reached. A failed project may leave no simple path to a refund.

Named users: early-stage startup and prototype teams and the backers funding them.

Milestones reduce backer risk because the full budget does not leave the vault at once.

The operator profits twice: an upfront platform cut (typically a few percent of the raise plus payment processing) and control — it can freeze or reject a campaign and decides when the creator is paid.

The intermediary, named · design rationale

The operator is custody plus discretionary payout

Before: Kickstarter-style

The platform holds the pledges, controls the payout flow and becomes the trusted intermediary for every failure case.

After: Bestcrow

The program holds funds, locks the schedule, gates later releases by backer votes and exposes individual refunds.

Why not a database? A DB admin can edit state and still controls the payout rails. Here the rule — not an operator — is the source of truth, and neither party can bypass it.

How it works · part 1

Terms are locked before the first pledge

Create draft→Add 2–5 tranches (≤50% each, =100%)→Seal & open funding
  • Funding window 7–183 days; overfunding allowed — no cap at the goal.
  • Each tranche's share, work period and recipients are fixed; no post-start edits.
  • The MVP requires no creator deposit. Anti-bot measures come later.
On-chain: create_draft · add_tranche · seal_termsSlide 4 / 10
The schedule is committed atomically. No one — not the creator, not us — can reshape the tranches after money is in. This is the "identical for every participant" the brief asks for.
How it works · part 2

Backers approve each release — the rule decides

Evidence → 7-day vote

Weight = final pledge. Approval needs >50% of all contributions. Exactly 50% or no votes = no.

One revision, then stop

First failure opens 30 days to improve, then a second 7-day vote. Second failure → termination.

An approved tranche is payable exactly once to a fixed recipient. Anyone may trigger the technical release once the rule is satisfied.

The program enforces the threshold and the windows — no admin picks winnersSlide 5 / 10
Votes measure approval, not product quality — we say that plainly. The point is that no admin picks winners; the program enforces the threshold and the windows.
How it works · failure is first-class

Every failure path is permissionless and pro-rata

  • Missed goal: no fee; each backer claims 100% of their own pledge.
  • Termination: the remaining pool is frozen and refunded proportionally to frozen pledge weight.
  • Double-claim guard: refunds/releases are exactly-once; a closed ledger can't be replayed.
  • Anyone can submit an eligible finalize/refund transaction — a timer never moves money by itself.

If a party disappears: the goal simply misses and refunds open; a rejected milestone terminates and refunds; approved tranches stay claimable. No single party can lock the money.

On-chain: finalize_funding · refund_for · exactly-once claimsSlide 6 / 10
This is the question judges ask: what if one party disappears? The answer is on-chain: a failed goal refunds, a rejected milestone terminates and refunds pro-rata, and no single party can block it permanently.

What is true today

What the program does — and the one honest limit

Enforced on-chain

Fixed goal, deadline and recipients · program-owned vault · permissionless finalization and refunds · exactly-once releases · disclosed upgrade authority.

The honest limit

The program enforces money, not worthiness or delivery. Backer votes express approval, not product quality. Previously paid funds can't be clawed back. Upgrade authority is disclosed, not hidden.
